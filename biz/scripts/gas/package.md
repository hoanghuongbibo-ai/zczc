## 2. Thumbnail Concepts

1. **Subject:** gas pump nozzle. **Visual:** nozzle dripping into a map of the Strait of Hormuz. **Emotion:** dread. **Text:** "$4.36"
2. **Subject:** two price lines. **Visual:** red oil line falling, gas line flat and high, with a rocket and a feather. **Emotion:** confusion, then anger. **Text:** "OIL ↓ GAS ↑?"
3. **Subject:** a family at the pump. **Visual:** a receipt reading "$860." **Emotion:** frustration. **Text:** "YOUR WAR BILL"
4. **Subject:** tanker in a narrow channel. **Visual:** a tanker on fire (archival, licensed) beside a gas sign. **Emotion:** high stakes. **Text:** "THIS IS WHY"

Avoid real company logos as the "villain." The gouging claim is an allegation.

## 6. Fact-Check Notes

1. **Khamenei's death (Ch. 1).** Very big claim. Sourced to Reuters video archive records. Confirm with a Reuters or AP text story before lock.
2. **"Largest oil supply shock on record" (Ch. 2).** Attributed to ABC News on screen. The IEA has used similar language ("largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market," per its March Oil Market Report as cited on Wikipedia). Pull the IEA report directly if you want to quote the IEA instead.
3. **Price gouging (Ch. 5).** Stated only as Trump's accusation, with the API and Chevron responses next to it. We found no public result of a DOJ investigation as of Oct 9. Re-check before publishing. Never say oil companies gouged.
4. **Rockets and feathers (Ch. 5).** The Borenstein-Cameron findings use 1980s data. The script says so. A St. Louis Fed piece (Aug 2026, "When Oil Prices Drop, Why Do Gasoline Prices Stay Elevated?") exists, but its page returned 404 during research. Worth adding if it can be retrieved.
5. **Household cost (Ch. 7, Our Take).** $750+ (Brown, gasoline and diesel, through Sep 7) and $860 (Zandi, includes jet fuel) are model-based estimates. The script presents them as a range of estimates, not a bill.
6. **Exxon "$160 million a day."** Our calculation: $14.5B / 91 days ≈ $159M. CNN's Jul 31 headline uses the same figure. Spoken as "roughly."
7. **Oil price moves.** Pre-war ~$70 and "nearly $120" are from NPR (Mar 11), describing the global benchmark. EIA puts the April monthly-average Brent peak around $117 ($85 June + $32). Don't state a single exact peak.
8. **Gas price peak.** We avoided naming one 2026 peak. Confirmed readings: $4.56 (May 21), $4.48 (Sep 24), September average $4.33. ABC's Oct 8 note that gas is "down about 21 cents over the past month" suggests a late-September high near $4.57. Unverified, so not used.
9. **War powers.** "20 votes" is from AP (Sep 25). The House passed a resolution at least twice (Jun 3; again in July per NPR). The Senate failed 50-49 on Sep 24.
10. **Kpler pre-war comparison.** "Back above pre-war" applies to Gulf crude exports on 14 days in September, not every day. The script says "by the end of September."
11. **Hormuz share of world oil.** "About a fifth" (NPR). Don't use the "21 miles" width in titles unless it is sourced (title option #5).
12. **"Under the Constitution, only Congress can declare [war]."** Article I, Section 8. Common knowledge, no source needed, but keep the wording exact.
13. **Volatile figures.** Gas, oil, Hormuz traffic and attack counts change daily. Refresh S6 and S7 numbers on recording day.
