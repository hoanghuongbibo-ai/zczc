# 01 — Content analysis: "Merer's Diary", cold open and chapter 1 (opening)

**Supplied material:** the cold open, plus chapter 1 up to "…one Greek tourist." That's about 480 words of narration, roughly **3:00 at 160 wpm** (3:02 cut). It's the first section of a 19–20 minute episode.
**Production defaults used:**
- tool-agnostic prompts, adapted to the specific tool once the user names one
- 16:9, 4K masters, 24 fps
- English narration and subtitles
- clips of 5–8 s each

## What the content says
1. **Immersive hook.** "You" wake up on a cargo boat below the Tura limestone quarries during the summer flood. Forty men sleep on deck. The team leader, Merer, writes one line: where the team spent the night.
2. **The reveal.** That writing survived. Archaeologists recovered it from a cave on the Red Sea coast about 4,500 years later. It's the oldest inscribed papyrus found, and its author helped supply the Great Pyramid.
3. **The question.** Forget whips and aliens. What did the builders do all day? Who fed them? Who healed them? Why did they do it?
4. **Chapter 1 context.** Khufu is a god-king who needs a monument for his journey to the sky. The pyramid is about 481 ft and about 2.3 million blocks, and it was the tallest building for about 3,800 years. Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the pyramid's construction. The traditional answer to who built it comes from a Greek writer, Herodotus.

## Thesis
For the first time, the builders speak for themselves through a logbook. The episode will replace myth (whips, aliens, Herodotus' numbers) with their own record.

## Strengths
- An unusually strong hook: second person, sensory, with one concrete object (the papyrus line) that pays off at once.
- A clear promise and a question the rest of the episode answers.
- Built-in visual set pieces: dawn on the flood, the quarry cliff, the writing close-up, the block on the river, the cave discovery, the pyramid at full height, and the timeline gag.

## Gaps and risks (details in 02)
| Issue | Why it matters | Proposed handling |
|---|---|---|
| "reed pen" | Old Kingdom scribes used a frayed **rush brush**; reed pens arrived with Greek writing much later. | Visuals show a rush brush and palette. Suggested VO fix: "a rush brush" (needs the user's OK). |
| "move a block … today, like most days" | The diary records round trips between Tura and Giza every few days, with many blocks per boatload, not one block a day. | Keep the drama, but show several blocks on the boat. Suggested VO fix: "move blocks of stone, each about as heavy as a pickup truck". |
| "It's July" | Inundation timing and the diary's entries fit summer, but the specific month is a reconstruction. | On-screen label: *RECONSTRUCTION*. |
| "oldest inscribed papyrus ever found" | It's accurate only with "so far", and blank papyrus is older (the 1st-dynasty tomb of Hemaka). | Suggested wording: "the oldest inscribed papyri found so far". |
| Khufu's appearance | The only securely identified statue of Khufu is a tiny ivory figurine, so we have no reliable likeness. | Show him stylised or in shadow, never as a "photo" portrait. |
| "for about 2,500 years the world's answer came from one Greek tourist" | Rhetorical. Herodotus (c. 430s BC) was the most influential source, but not the only one. | Acceptable as narration; the subtitle notes Herodotus, *Histories* 2.124. |
